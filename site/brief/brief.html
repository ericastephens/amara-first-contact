Amara First Contact: right care from the first door.

Hai, Kilimanjaro, Tanzania
November 2026

An offline AI app that helps accredited drug shops (ADDOs) and community health workers in rural Tanzania triage and refer mothers and children to the right clinic, on a low-cost Android phone with no internet.

Problem

4.4 / 10,000

Health workers per 10,000 people in Tanzania; WHO recommends 44.5.

First stop

31%

Of people go to a drug shop first when ill, more than to a public facility.

Missed

60.7% vs 1.6%

Severe fevers diagnosed as malaria vs. those that were malaria (northern Tanzania).

Solution

She speaks Swahili

A 660 KB intent model on the phone maps her words to a fixed list of symptoms and exposures. Below 40% confidence it says "Not sure".

Rules set urgency

Sourced Tanzanian and WHO guideline rules decide: Go now, refer today, or within 3 days. The dispenser confirms every flag.

She gets an SMS

Clinic, time and code, no health details. Her note and draft ICD-10 codes reach the clinician first; anonymous counts flag outbreaks.

Business model

Free for mothers. Paid by health programmes. NGO and donor programmes first (setup + per site), then per completed referral, district licences and insurers.

$24k

A year per district: 100 sites × 20 referrals/month × $1. Illustrative.

$1 vs ~$170

Referral vs. one m-mama emergency transport (Munishi 2023).

Market

~$4M /yr

Tanzania potential: ~170 district councils × $24k. Illustrative.

2.4M

Births a year in Tanzania; 9,000+ accredited drug shops.

Beachhead: Hai District → Kilimanjaro → Tanzania → Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda.

Why we win

Fully offline AI

Triage needs no data or signal; only the SMS needs a network.

Why we win

At the drug shop counter

Catches mothers where they go first, before an emergency. Complements m-mama and Afya-Tek.

Why we win

Swahili built for clinics

An intent model for how mothers describe symptoms and exposures, with sourced rules on top.
